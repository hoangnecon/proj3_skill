# Premise ideation — how to invent uncanny, curiosity-exploding rules

Never settle for a dry tally, a lecture disguised as magic, or a generic "emotion turns into element X" metaphor. Viewers click and watch to the end when the opening sentence breaks a basic assumption of daily life and immediately poses a puzzle: *"Wait — how would people even survive or eat dinner in that town?"*

## 1. Four generative lenses

Use these four abstract lenses to generate fresh world rules set in everyday northern Vietnamese spaces (`ngõ`, `chung cư`, `chợ`, `trường học`).

### Lens 1 — Perceptual / physical axiom inversion
Take one invisible assumption humans rely on every second (how light, sound, gravity, perspective, reflection, or momentum behaves) and invert or literalize one rule of physics.
- *How it generates stories:* Daily habits break instantly without needing a villain. People invent comical adaptations (`sv-20` props/signs) just to commute or buy breakfast.
- *Corpus calibration:* VoidCast `S2k-6xQWM34` (11.4M views — tripping pulls you toward who you love most rather than the floor); `@rotoichoi` `RPmvj_wCeR0` (3.7M views — winter speech freezes solid in mid-air).

### Lens 2 — Everyday object with a strict state/memory law
Pick one ordinary object (`tờ báo`, `cái thang`, `hàng rào`, `bức ảnh`, `ổ khóa`) and bind it to an uncanny `input -> state -> output` rule.
- *How it generates stories:* **Never stop at the linear use.** Ask: *what happens if you reverse its orientation, feed it an edge-case input, or obey its literal wording instead of its spirit?*
- *Corpus calibration:* `@rotoichoi` `41JbqNKfDNQ` (7.0M views — newspaper prints tomorrow's news) and `e6r3JkN8OzM` (7.0M views — anything crossing the fence disappears).

### Lens 3 — Binary split humanity (symbiotic / opposing halves)
Divide everyone in town, school, or one household into **Group A** and **Group B**, where A's sensory/physical rule is the mirror inverse of B's — so neither half can safely navigate crisis alone.
- *How it generates stories:* Instant world-building gags at home and school, plus built-in **symbiotic climax**: Group A and Group B must combine their incompatible constraints into one synchronized move.
- *Key guardrails:* Keep both halves equally constrained (never "cool power vs useless power"), and verify how both halves eat, sleep, and move without breaking logic.

### Lens 4 — The backwards personal glitch
Everyone in the alley obeys a quirky personal rule. Most neighbours draw flashy, convenient rules (`sv-01` world); the narrator draws the single glitch that looks purely humiliating or counterproductive — until Act 3 reveals it is the **only** mechanical tool that fits the climax lock.
- *Strict bans:* Never use `"your birth name is your superpower"`, never hand out generic fantasy spells, and never build a villain defeated by brute force.

## 2. Reverse-design protocol (design Act 3 before s1)

Do **not** invent a clever `s1` hook first and scramble for an ending later — that creates external-disaster twists and preachy morals. Design **backwards**:

1. **Invent the Lateral Rule-Hack first:** How can the rule's *own literal physics or edge case* solve an impossible crisis while extracting a real cost?
2. **Build the Double-Bind Dilemma:** Construct the trap where *Option A* (using the rule normally) destroys what the hero wants to save, and *Option B* (not using it) guarantees disaster before the clock runs out.
3. **Design the Personal Handicap & Foil (`Knot 1`):** Give `"Tôi"` an Act-1 weakness caused by this exact rule, paired with a Foil character whose seemingly opposite habit hides an unspoken reason (`Past-Flip` target).
4. **Embed the Plant (`s5–s9`):** Place the specific prop, layout constraint, or secondary clause casually in Act 1 so Act 3 never relies on luck.
5. **Write `s1–s3` last:**
   - `s1` (`≤ 16–18` words): State the rule with zero preamble.
   - `s2–s3`: Show the visual state (`sv-20`) + immediately slam the **Surface-Loophole Blocker** (*"Leaving the room doesn't reset it; faking X does nothing"*).

## 3. Sanity checklist before pitching G1
- **The Stranger Test:** If you say ONLY `s1` out loud, does someone ask *"Wait, then what happens when..."* within 2 seconds? If they shrug *"Oh, like a metaphor"*, discard it.
- **No-Escape Test:** Is the protagonist forced to interact with the rule even if they try to stay home and do nothing?
- **Optics & Physics Check:** Check sightlines, gravity, conservation of action, and sleeping/eating edge cases before writing `beats.md`.
